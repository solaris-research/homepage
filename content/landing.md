## Intro

An AI safety research lab studying how advanced AI systems behave, why
their safety properties sometimes fail, and what that means for a world
that increasingly depends on them.

## Mission

Solaris Research is based at ETH Zurich. Our work is technical: we
investigate the internals of frontier models and the conditions under
which their safety properties hold or break down. The questions are
narrow; the stakes are not.

## Research thesis

*Our bet* is that safety failures are tied to low-dimensional structure
inside models — structure that can be recovered mechanistically. If we
can find it, we can trace how it drifts under adaptation. And if we can
trace it, we can intervene: steering vectors, probe-guided losses, and
circuit-level analysis applied *during* training, not after.

We also believe the field needs a stronger evidential foundation. Too
many safety claims rest on narrow benchmarks, unreproduced results, or
anthropomorphic language that outruns the evidence. Rigorous evaluation
— with careful operationalisation, sufficient scale, and tests for
alternative explanations — is not overhead; it is the work itself. This
demands a multi-disciplinary approach: drawing on machine learning,
interpretability, cognitive science, and epistemology.

The hardest version of this problem is *agentic*. When models call
tools, coordinate with other agents, and act across extended horizons,
failures compound in ways single-turn evaluations cannot surface. Safety
is not solved in a chat window.

## We work on

- How safety-relevant representations form and degrade across pretraining, fine-tuning, and post-deployment adaptation — and what predicts drift before it reaches production.
- Tools, datasets, and evaluations that catch misalignment during training — mechanistic probes, steering interventions, and stress tests at adaptation checkpoints.
- Active interpretability: bringing steering vectors, circuit analysis, and probe-guided losses into the training loop, early enough to prevent problems before they consolidate.
- Agentic and multi-agent systems — correlated failures, collusion, and alignment under realistic deployment pressure, studied directly rather than by analogy to chat.
- Open benchmarks, reproducible pipelines, and preparedness tools that others can check, build on, and extend.

## Work with us

We are looking for technically strong, safety-driven students and
collaborators. If you want your work to matter, get in touch.

- **Thesis & semester projects** — work on real frontier safety problems, co-supervised across ETH labs, toward conference publications and open-source artefacts.
- **Research visits & independent projects** — bring your own safety question; we provide compute, mentorship, and collaborators.
- **Labs & industry** — if you deploy models and care about what happens to safety after fine-tuning, work with us to discover problems early.
